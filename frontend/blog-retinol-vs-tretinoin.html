Retinol vs Retinoids vs Tretinoin: Which One Is Right for Your Skin?

Nov 2, 2026 · @T2

Retinol, retinoids and tretinoin get used interchangeably online, but they are not interchangeable on your skin. They are all derivatives of vitamin A. Retinoid skincare isn't just “retinol or tretinoin.” Dermatology recognises a full ladder of vitamin A derivatives, each one closer to, or further from, the active form skin cells respond to. Knowing where you sit on that ladder is what keeps you from quitting after one irritated week.

Retinol
An over-the-counter vitamin A derivative that converts twice inside skin cells before it activates: milder, slower, more forgiving.
Retinoids
The umbrella term for every vitamin A derivative, from gentle retinol to potent prescription compounds, that speeds up cell turnover and supports collagen.
Tretinoin
A prescription-strength retinoid (pure retinoic acid) that skips the conversion step and binds directly to skin receptors: the fastest-acting and most potent of the three.

So what do they actually do?

When we put retinol on our face, it sends a signal to the skin that there is a wound to heal, so the skin makes more collagen. It basically tricks the skin into making more collagen. Over time, the result is 44 percent less wrinkled skin compared with skin not using it.

Who should use which?

  • Sensitive or beginner skin: start with retinol at 0.1–0.3%, two to three nights a week, building up slowly.
  • Tolerant, experienced skin: tretinoin, prescribed and monitored by a dermatologist, goes deeper but brings more redness, peeling and dryness while skin adjusts.

What the evidence shows

A 2021 clinical review in the Journal of Cutaneous Medicine and Surgery lays out this exact ladder: retinyl esters convert to retinol, which converts to retinaldehyde, which converts to retinoic acid. Because retinaldehyde needs only one conversion step instead of two, it's considered meaningfully more potent than retinol. The same review notes that retinoic acid itself can be hundreds of times more potent than its cosmetic precursors, which is also why it carries the highest chance of irritation.

A 2006 review in Clinical Interventions in Aging found tretinoin to be the most potent, most-studied retinoid for photoaging, with retinol offering similar but slower, gentler effects. A 2014 pharmacology review called tretinoin “the best tested retinoid to reverse photoaged skin.” That is why dermatology still treats tretinoin as the gold standard for anti-ageing: not hype, but decades of clinical evidence.

Before you start

Skip all three during pregnancy, and pair any retinoid with daily SPF since they raise sun sensitivity. Give it 12+ weeks before judging results. Early flaking and redness are normal, not failure.

Skin care is a marathon, not a sprint.
